## Overview

UCR RISE 2025 research with Alec Peck and Dr. Shawn Westerdale on the DarkSide-20k dark matter experiment.

Simulating how light spreads through the detector's Outer Veto with Geant4 takes hours of CPU time for each interaction position. I built a neural-network surrogate that predicts the same light pattern in milliseconds.

- **Input:** interaction position (ρ, φ, z), with φ encoded as sin/cos.
- **Output:** the light seen by each of the 128 photodetector units.

## Model

A 3-layer ReLU MLP with two heads:

- a **softmax** head for the shape of the distribution across channels,
- a **Softplus** head for the total amount of light.

**Loss:** KL divergence on the shape plus 0.1 × MSE on log(1 + total). Trained with Adam (lr 1e-3) and early stopping on an 80/10/10 train/validation/test split.

**Data:** a coarse Geant4 light map with 55,188 voxels, of which 1,463 are filled.

## Results

- **0.993 cosine similarity** on held-out channel distributions.
- Total light was harder: MAE of about 2e-3 against a median total of 3.6e-3. The main reason is how sparse the light map is.

## Inverse model

Later in the project we worked on the reverse problem: predicting the 3D interaction position from the 128 channels. The first version overfit. Preprocessing changes and regularization fixed it:

| Metric            | Before | After |
| ----------------- | ------ | ----- |
| Validation loss   | 0.292  | 0.074 |
| Cosine similarity | 0.780  | 0.927 |

I coordinated the ML workstream and assigned owners for preprocessing, modeling, results, and documentation.
